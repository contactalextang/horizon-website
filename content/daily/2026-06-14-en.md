The ban applies to all statistical products from both the Census Bureau and the Bureau of Economic Analysis, replacing differential privacy with raw data releases; critics warn this raises re‑identification risks, while supporters argue it restores data accuracy.

**Background**: Differential privacy is a mathematically rigorous framework that allows aggregate statistics to be released while limiting the risk of exposing individual data subjects, often implemented by adding calibrated random noise—referred to as noise infusion—to the data. The U.S. Census Bureau has previously used noise infusion as part of its disclosure avoidance systems to balance data utility with privacy protection. The recent order removes this safeguard, opting instead for publishing data without the added noise.

The piece highlights technical aspects such as Unicode’s bidirectional algorithm (UAX #9), OpenType contextual shaping features (isol, init, medi, fina, liga, ccmp, etc.), cursive joining rules, and typographic justification techniques like Kashida, while noting limitations in many editors and rendering pipelines.

It also uses bidirectional text, where Arabic runs right-to-left while numbers and embedded Latin text run left-to-right, necessitating the Unicode Bidirectional Algorithm. Diacritics, ligatures, and justification methods such as Kashida further complicate rendering, relying on OpenType features and proper font support.

## [Reverse‑Engineered Adder Circuit in Intel 8087 Floating‑Point Coprocessor Revealed](https://www.righto.com/2026/06/intel-8087-adder-reverse-engineered.html) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Understanding the 8087's adder sheds light on early floating-point hardware design, influencing later x87 implementations and the IEEE 754 standard, and provides valuable insights for historians and engineers studying vintage computer architecture. The adder is partitioned into 4-bit slices, each using a Manchester carry chain for carry generation and a carry-skip circuit to speed up propagation, forming a carry-lookahead adder without a traditional full-adder per bit. The design lacks on‑die decoupling capacitance and relies on metal layers for power delivery.

hackernews · Hacker News RSS · Jun 13, 16:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48519011)

**Background**: The Intel 8087, released in 1980, was the first floating-point coprocessor for the 8086/8088 CPUs, providing hardware support for floating-point operations and influencing the IEEE 754 standard. It lacks a hardware multiplier, using CORDIC algorithms instead, and its internal datapath includes an 80‑bit wide fraction section with adders, shifters, and constant storage. Reverse‑engineering such historic chips helps preserve knowledge of early VLSI design techniques.
