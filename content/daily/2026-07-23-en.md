## [Terrence Tao Uses ChatGPT to Explore Jacobian Conjecture Counterexample](https://chatgpt.com/share/6a5fdc7a-d6f8-83e8-bbea-8deb42cfed56) ⭐️ 8.0/10

In a shared ChatGPT conversation, Terrence Tao examined a potential counterexample to the Jacobian conjecture by asking the model pointed, iterative questions about a specially structured polynomial. The conversation illustrates that even leading mathematicians find large language models useful for probing notoriously difficult problems, suggesting a new avenue for AI‑assisted research in pure mathematics. Tao’s prompting strategy involved repeatedly asking for simplifications and focusing on the polynomial’s specific structure, rather than relying on brute‑force search.

hackernews · Hacker News RSS · Jul 22, 17:30 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49010345)

**Background**: The Jacobian conjecture posits that a polynomial map from ℝⁿ to ℝⁿ with a non‑zero constant Jacobian determinant must have a polynomial inverse. First formulated in the late 19th century, it has become a notorious problem in algebraic geometry, attracting many false proofs and appearing as problem #16 in Smale’s list of mathematical problems for the next century. While the two‑dimensional case remains unresolved, recent work using large language models (such as the Claude model counterexample reported in July 2026) has disproved the conjecture for dimensions greater than two.

## [Everyone Should Know SIMD](https://mitchellh.com/writing/everyone-should-know-simd) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Mitchell Hashimoto's blog post introduces SIMD fundamentals, its performance advantages, and practical tips for effective use, accompanied by a Hacker News discussion highlighting data‑oriented design and real‑world examples. Understanding SIMD helps developers achieve significant speedups in compute‑intensive workloads, and the discussion shows how combining SIMD with good data layout can yield better real‑world performance. The post notes that SIMD works best when data is contiguous and aligned, warns against premature optimization without profiling, and cites compiler auto‑vectorization as a fallback.

hackernews · Hacker News RSS · Jul 22, 17:48 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49010648)

**Background**: SIMD (Single Instruction, Multiple Data) is a parallel computing technique where a single CPU instruction operates on multiple data points simultaneously, enabling vectorized operations. Together, these concepts help developers write high‑performance code by aligning computation with memory access patterns.

## [GigaToken achieves ~1000x faster tokenization via SIMD and caching.](https://github.com/marcelroed/gigatoken/) ⭐️ 7.0/10

GigaToken, a new open‑source library, reports roughly a 1000× speedup in language model tokenization by heavily optimizing the pretokenization step with SIMD instructions and aggressive caching. While tokenization accounts for less than 0.1% of inference latency, the massive speedup greatly accelerates offline data preprocessing for training corpora, reducing iteration time and cost for large‑scale NLP projects. The library replaces the usual regex‑based pretokenizer with a branch‑less SIMD implementation, caches pretoken‑to‑id lookups, and reports consistent performance across modern x86 and ARM CPUs and various tokenizers such as BPE and WordPiece.

hackernews · Hacker News RSS · Jul 22, 17:20 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49010167)

**Background**: In language model pipelines, tokenization converts raw text into numerical IDs that the model consumes; the first step, pretokenization, splits text into words or subword units often using regular expressions. SIMD (Single Instruction, Multiple Data) allows a CPU to apply the same operation to many data elements in parallel, enabling high‑throughput processing when branches are minimized. Although tokenization is typically a tiny fraction of total inference latency, it dominates the cost of preprocessing large text corpora for training.
