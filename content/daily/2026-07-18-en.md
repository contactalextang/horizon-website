Astronomers using the James Webb Space Telescope have detected an atmosphere around the rocky exoplanet LHS 1140b, which orbits within the habitable zone of its red dwarf star. This detection marks the first time an atmosphere has been found on a rocky, Earth‑sized world in a habitable zone, boosting the prospects for identifying potentially habitable exoplanets and guiding future biosignature searches. The atmosphere was identified via transit spectroscopy with JWST, showing spectral features consistent with a dense, possibly nitrogen‑rich envelope; the planet’s mass is about 5.6 Earth masses and it orbits 0.095 AU from its star every 24.7 days.

The habitable zone is defined as the range of orbital distances where surface temperatures allow liquid water, a key ingredient for life as we know it. Astronomers study exoplanet atmospheres using transit spectroscopy, which measures how starlight filters through a planet’s atmosphere during a transit, revealing absorption lines of gases such as helium, water vapor, or methane.
