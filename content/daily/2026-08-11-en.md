## [Parametron: 1950s Japanese computer using magnetic logic instead of tubes or transistors](https://ethw.org/Milestones:Parametron,_1954) ⭐️ 7.0/10

The Parametron, a 1950s Japanese computing technology invented by Eiichi Goto in 1954, used magnetic coupling in ferrite cores instead of vacuum tubes or transistors, powering early computers like NEC's NEAC-1101 completed in March 1958. The Parametron represents a forgotten but viable alternative path in computing history, demonstrating that early digital computers could be built using magnetic logic, which influenced Japan's indigenous computer development and reduced reliance on foreign technology. The NEAC-1101 used 3,600 parametrons, supported 7-digit decimal floating-point operations, had 29 instruction types, and employed a single-turn transformer coupling system independently developed by NEC to improve reliability over Goto's original design.

hackernews · xeonmc · Aug 10, 10:29 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49241846)

**Background**: The Parametron is a logic element based on parametric excitation in ferrite cores, invented by Eiichi Goto in 1954 as a tube- and transistor-free switching device. It operated by varying magnetic flux through nonlinear ferrite materials, enabling signal amplification and logic operations via magnetic coupling between adjacent units via signal wires threaded through ferrite cores. This technology emerged during the 1950s when multiple alternative logic families — including cryotrons, tunnel diodes, and magnetic core logic — were explored as potential replacements for vacuum tubes before transistors became dominant.
